# Reusing other people's videos without hurting monetization

This is practical guidance from how YouTube's policies are generally described, not legal advice, and not a
guarantee. Policies change: check YouTube's current "reused content" guidance in the Partner Program rules.

## Two different things

1. **Permission (copyright).** A Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) license says you may reuse and edit the
   video if you credit the creator. That is what the scout verifies.
2. **Monetization (YouTube Partner Program).** Even with permission, YouTube can limit or remove monetization on a
   channel whose content is mostly someone else's material with little added by you. A license does not exempt you.
   This is judged for the channel as a whole, not clip by clip.

## What lowers the risk

- **Add your own value.** The more of the finished video that is yours (your voiceover, commentary, explanation,
  analysis, a reaction, a comparison, a "what this means" segment), the safer. A bare clip with only a title is the riskiest.
- **Keep it a minority.** Original videos should stay most of the channel. Reused clips are best as a small share.
  Do not post many clips from the same source in a row.
- **Combine and contextualize.** One video that brings together two or three clips on a topic with your framing
  beats three separate raw clips.
- **Credit every time.** Paste the CC BY credit from the clip's `.txt` into the description (and the pinned comment).
- **Check for third-party material.** A CC BY license only covers what the uploader owns. Music, film clips and
  stock footage inside the video may belong to someone else and can trigger Content ID claims. Skip videos whose
  description credits licensed music or footage.
- **Keep clips short and meaningful.** 25-60 seconds around one idea, not long stretches.
- **Do not imply endorsement.** The credit text is written to avoid that; do not add claims that the creator
  supports you.
- **Consider the channel.** If the main channel is monetized and valuable, test reused clips on a separate channel or in a small
  number of uploads first.

## What stays risky

- Clips with no commentary posted in bulk.
- Videos that are not CC BY (the credit file warns you: get written permission first).
- Re-uploading a whole talk, or a long portion, instead of a clip.
